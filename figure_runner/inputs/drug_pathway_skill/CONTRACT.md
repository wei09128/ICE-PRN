# Fixed continuous cancer-pathway prediction evaluation v1

Authorized on 22 October 2026 after the four-gene both-success reference made the previous enrichment endpoint imprecise. Exploratory development-data follow-up; not preregistered confirmation. This specification is written before inspecting outcomes for the new endpoint.

Question: does learned chemical-response transfer beat the destination training mean within the already fixed destination-matched cancer-pathway readouts, and does its skill exceed that over the complement?

Use all 4,185 original chemical identities, the original MCF7↔PC3 technical halves, outer folds, equal identity weights, per-ID aggregation, landmark axes, means and model settings. Reuse saved errors if sufficiently granular; otherwise replay the original fits without tuning and require all 100 original-loss checks before interpretation. No new compound exclusion or pathway selection.

Fixed masks: the original KEGG prostate-cancer membership in destination PC3 (23 measured landmarks), and breast-cancer membership in destination MCF7 (33). Complements are all other original measured landmarks. Copy source mask files unchanged and verify membership/axis mappings before scoring. No disease-reversal reference or therapeutic ranking is used.

Compute each method's mean squared error per chemical identity and readout group, averaging both technical orientations and IDs within identity exactly as in the original benchmark. Aggregate losses equally across identities BEFORE forming skill = 1 − model_MSE / destination_mean_MSE. Do not average per-identity percentage skills.

Four primary estimands: two directional learned skills within the fixed pathway masks; two directional differences between pathway skill and complement skill. Ten thousand shared chemical-identity bootstrap draws (seed 2026102213) retain all methods and both directions together. Fits, controls, folds, masks and technical halves remain fixed. Report conditional 95% intervals and approximate Bonferroni-adjusted 98.75% marginal intervals for the four primary endpoints. Do not resample genes as independent units. Zero, copying and affine summaries are descriptive comparators; no selected-drug efficacy inference.

Secondary: per-identity absolute loss improvement over the destination mean, within pathway and complement, to show heterogeneity. No per-drug significance calls, discovery list or treatment ranking. Retain all estimates regardless of sign or interval, including null/negative results. Do not replace frozen prior outputs. Plot the full fixed comparison regardless of outcome; a main-figure decision must depend on relevance and precision rather than significance.

Interpretation: pathway skill measures predictive gain within annotated readouts. Pathway-minus-complement skill measures relative predictive gain under fixed masks; it does not establish disease causality, activity specific to these cell lines, phenotype reversal, therapeutic efficacy, lineage-independent effects or preferential correction after expression/reliability matching. Landmark coverage and technical splits limit interpretation. Bootstrap intervals omit refitting, mask selection and independent biological-replication uncertainty.

Implementation clarification before new uncertainty estimation: the prior local identity×gene loss cache retains destination mean, copying and learned errors, but not affine or zero errors. Cache mode uses those three saved methods for the fixed primary endpoints and descriptive copying comparator; affine/zero are omitted explicitly rather than reconstructed. If cache mode is unavailable, an unchanged 20-fit replay evaluates all five original methods and requires 100 anchor checks. Both modes must reproduce archived pathway and complement point estimates before bootstrap release. New conditional intervals remain unavailable in this environment until per-identity losses are supplied. Previously archived continuous point estimates were found after this specification was written and are development-exposed; this follow-up is exploratory.
