# Active figure captions

Figure 1. Genetic and chemical response transfer. A, Skill against the destination training mean for predicting no change (open squares) and copying (filled circles). B, Affine and rank-16 cross-gene skill, including genetic fits on normalized counts. Analyses include 1,874 genetic targets and 4,185 chemical identities. Blue, K562→RPE1 or MCF7→PC3; orange, reverse. Interventions are held out and opposite technical halves scored; genetic scoring excludes own-target readouts. Intervals: conditional 95% cluster bootstrap, 10,000 draws, with fits and controls fixed.

Figure 2. Transfer scores depend on the comparator and response magnitude. A, Copying has identical whole-response MSE in both genetic directions, but destination-mean loss differs, so percentage skill differs. B, For nonzero scalar destination response y, copying beats predicting no change only when 0 < s/y < 2. C, Copy skill against zero for Hallmark E2F and G2M component responses on lists ranked by predicted risk (source magnitude) and retained from 20% to 100% of targets; vertical scales differ. D, Skill for the lowest-risk 20% (375 targets per task). Intervals in A, C and D are conditional 95% target-bootstrap intervals (10,000 draws), with fits, rankings and controls fixed.

Figure 3. Biological associations of learned transfer. A, External p53-hit coverage by prediction outcome; labels give hits/cases. B, p53-hit minus below-threshold rescue-rate gaps before and after matching; open diamonds are exploratory directional estimates. C, Learned skill within four identity-related programs minus skill over complementary readouts. D, The same contrast for cancer-pathway and estrogen-response readouts, against complements (circles) or matched references (diamonds); open markers indicate inadequate matching balance. Parentheses give measured gene counts. Intervals: conditional 95% in A–B, adjusted 99.375% across eight C contrasts, 98.75% for original pathway and estrogen contrasts in D, and 97.5% for its two matched cancer-pathway contrasts. Bootstrap draws: 20,000 in A and 10,000 otherwise, resampling targets, matching components or chemical identities as appropriate. Fits, masks and matches are fixed. Matching diagnostics and endpoint families are detailed in the Supporting Methods.

Figure 4. Correction capacity and p53-associated readouts. A, Held-out genetic skill across preset ranks; open diamonds mark the original rank-16 model. B, Gain over affine within 134 p53 readouts minus gain within 7,090 complementary readouts. C, Affine (open) and original rank-16 cross-gene (filled) skill within p53 readouts (direction colour) and complements (grey), after ribosome or other-target knockdown. For 72 ribosome targets, p53 skill rises from 11.5% to 50.2% toward RPE1 and from 10.3% to 27.5% toward K562. D, Ribosome minus nonmember difference in preferential p53 gain: +9.61 points [6.95, 12.01] forward and +1.00 [0.17, 1.89] reverse. Nonmembers comprise 1,796 targets; six unmapped targets are excluded. Own-target readouts are excluded. Intervals use 10,000 conditional target-cluster draws: 95% in A, adjusted 99.583% across 12 contrasts in B and 97.5% across two contrasts in D. C shows descriptive points; D stratifies resampling by target group. Directions were not formally compared, and measurement properties were not matched.

Figure S1. p53-hit coverage by transfer direction. External A549 p53-hit coverage across the four outcome groups, pooled and separately by direction. Error bars are exploratory 95% intervals from 20,000 target-gene bootstrap draws, keeping both directions together. The RPE1→K562 both-succeeded group contains only 18 cases (1 hit), limiting precision.

Conditional 95% intervals use 10,000 shared target-cluster draws, fixing fits, controls and masks. Own-target readouts are excluded. Relative program-versus-complement contrasts appear in Figure 3C.

Figure S3. Absolute p53 readout skill and secondary ribosomal contrasts.
