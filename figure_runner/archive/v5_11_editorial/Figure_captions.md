Figure 1. Genetic and chemical response transfer. A, Skill against the destination training mean for predicting no change (open squares) and copying (filled circles), across 1,874 genetic targets and 4,185 chemical identities. B, Affine and rank-16 cross-gene skill, including genetic fits on normalized counts. Blue, K562→RPE1 or MCF7→PC3; orange, reverse. Models hold out interventions and score opposite technical halves; genetic scoring excludes own-target readouts. Intervals are conditional 95% cluster-bootstrap intervals (10,000 draws), fixing fits and controls. Technical halves do not establish biological replication.

Figure 2. Transfer scores depend on the comparator and response magnitude. A, Copying has identical whole-response MSE in both genetic directions, but destination-mean loss differs, so percentage skill differs. B, For nonzero scalar destination response y, copying beats predicting no change only when 0 < s/y < 2. C, Copy skill against zero for Hallmark E2F and G2M component responses on lists ranked by predicted risk (source magnitude) and retained from 20% to 100% of targets; vertical scales differ. D, Skill for the lowest-risk 20% (375 targets per task). Intervals in A, C and D are conditional 95% target-bootstrap intervals (10,000 draws), with fits, rankings and controls fixed.

Figure 3. Biological associations of learned transfer. A, External p53-hit coverage by prediction outcome. B, p53-hit minus below-threshold rescue-rate differences before and after matching; open diamonds show exploratory directional estimates. C, Learned skill within four external identity-related programs minus skill over complementary readouts; parentheses give program sizes. D, Learned skill within destination-matched KEGG cancer-pathway readouts (left) and pathway minus complement skill (right), across all 4,185 chemical identities: prostate cancer toward PC3 (23 landmarks) and breast cancer toward MCF7 (33). Intervals are conditional bootstrap intervals: 95% in A--B, with 20,000 draws for p53 coverage and 10,000 otherwise; approximate Bonferroni-adjusted 99.375% in C and 98.75% in D (10,000 draws each). D resamples chemical identities; A--C resample targets or matching components. D uses the destination mean as comparator, averaging losses equally across identities before calculating skill. All four D endpoints are retained. Programs and pathways have incomplete coverage and unmatched abundance and repeatability. These analyses do not establish p53 mediation, identity causality, disease specificity or therapeutic efficacy.

Figure 4. Capacity of the learned correction and the readouts it improves. A, Held-out genetic skill across preset correction ranks; open diamonds mark the original rank-16 model. B, Fraction of the rank-32 gain over affine captured at each rank. C, Skill within 134 measured p53 target-gene readouts (filled) and the 7,090 other readouts (open), by rank and direction. D, Gain over affine within p53 target readouts minus gain within other readouts. Own-target readouts are excluded throughout. Intervals are conditional target-cluster bootstrap intervals (10,000 draws): 95% in A and C, and Bonferroni-adjusted 99.583% across the 12 rank--direction contrasts in D. Rank directions are ordered by source variance, not destination relevance. These exploratory analyses do not identify a biological rank, test a difference between directions or establish a learned p53 mechanism.

Figure S1. p53-hit coverage by transfer direction. External A549 p53-hit coverage across the four outcome groups, pooled and separately by direction. Error bars are exploratory 95% intervals from 20,000 target-gene bootstrap draws, keeping both directions together. The RPE1→K562 both-succeeded group contains only 18 cases (1 hit), limiting precision.

Figure S2. Learned prediction over fixed external-program readouts. A, Learned MSE reduction against the destination mean within four external gene sets; parentheses give measured readout counts. B, Program skill minus skill over other unambiguously mapped readouts. Intervals use 10,000 target-cluster draws: conditional 95% in A and Bonferroni-adjusted 99.375% marginal intervals in B. Fits, controls and masks are fixed; own-target readouts are excluded. Programs have incomplete coverage and unmatched abundance and repeatability. These endpoints do not establish biological replication or identity-specific correction.

Figure S3. Pooled chemical-response opposition across cancer cell lines. Both response halves were averaged before scoring all 4,185 identities. A, Cumulative negative-Spearman score distributions; positive scores indicate opposition, and legend values are medians. B, Paired sign patterns. Opposite signs occurred for 41.6% using PC3/RWPE1 and 40.7% using prostate tissue, versus 46.3% and 46.2% expected under independence preserving each line’s positive-score proportion. The MCF7 reference is fixed. Categories use strict zero thresholds without a minimum-effect requirement. Reference and gene-mask differences limit comparability; these patterns do not establish cancer specificity, a generic mechanism or independent validation.


Figure S4. Destination-program readouts in chemical transfer. A, Learned skill within Hallmark early (30 landmarks) and late (34) estrogen-response readouts minus skill over all other readouts or over readouts matched on vehicle-control expression and technical repeatability in both lines, for PC3→MCF7. B, KEGG prostate-cancer (MCF7→PC3) and breast-cancer (PC3→MCF7) readouts against all other readouts (original contrasts, as in Fig. 3D) and against matched readouts. Open markers indicate inadequate matching balance; those estimates are sensitivity analyses only. Intervals are conditional identity-cluster bootstrap intervals (10,000 draws): Bonferroni-adjusted 98.75% for the four estrogen contrasts and the original pathway contrasts, and 97.5% for the two matched pathway contrasts. These exploratory follow-ups were specified after the original pathway results were known and do not establish estrogen-receptor or disease-specific mechanisms.

Figure S5. Ribosomal knockdowns and p53 target readouts. A, Skill within the 134 p53 target readouts (direction colour) and the 7,090 other readouts (grey) with affine correction (open) and the original rank-16 cross-gene model (filled), for 72 KEGG ribosome targets and 1,796 other annotated targets. B, Ribosome minus other targets in preferential p53 gain over affine (p53-readout gain minus other-readout gain), original rank-16 model. C, The same contrast across the common-basis rank grid. Own-target readouts are excluded. Intervals are conditional target-cluster bootstrap intervals (10,000 draws, stratified by group): Bonferroni-adjusted 97.5% in B (two contrasts) and 99.583% in C (12 contrasts). Response magnitude, cell recovery and repeatability were not matched; nonmembers are not stress-negative controls. This exploratory contrast does not establish p53 mediation or a difference between directions.
